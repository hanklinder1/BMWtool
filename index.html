BMW Information Classification (ICL)

BMW classifies every data asset using a Confidentiality-first process with four levels. This tool acts as a pre-screening step — it produces a suggested ICL level that must be confirmed by a qualified BMW risk analyst. The Maximum Principle ensures that if any single attribute scores critically high, the entire dataset is escalated to the next classification level.

Step 1
CIA Impact Ratings per Attribute
Each attribute is rated on three CIA dimensions (C, I, A), each 0 to 1. These reflect how severely that attribute threatens each dimension if observed.
  • 0.0 — No impact
  • 0.3–0.5 — Moderate impact
  • 0.7–1.0 — High impact
Step 2
CIA Impact Factors
Each attribute has three independent CIA factors (0–1). They answer: "If fully observed, how severely does it threaten each CIA dimension?"
  • C (Confidentiality) — Exposes proprietary information?
  • I (Integrity) — Enables data manipulation?
  • A (Availability) — Reveals dependency?
Step 3
Global CIA Weights (α, β, γ)
Three weights reflect BMW's organizational prioritization. Normalized to sum to 1 before computation.
  • α (Confidentiality) = 0.60 — highest priority
  • β (Integrity) = 0.25 — medium priority
  • γ (Availability) = 0.15 — lower priority
Step 4
Per-Attribute Score
Score_i = αn × C_i + βn × I_i + γn × A_i
Step 5
Final Risk Score
Risk Score = 100 × (αn × C_avg + βn × I_avg + γn × A_avg)
Step 6
ICL Classification & Maximum Principle
The score maps to BMW's four ICL levels:
  • 0–25 → Not Public (up to ~$10,000)
  • 26–45 → Confidential (up to ~$1,000,000)
  • 46–70 → Strictly Confidential (up to ~$100,000,000)
  • 71–100 → Secret (over $100,000,000)

Maximum Principle: If any attribute's CIA value ≥ 0.85, the entire dataset escalates to the next ICL level.
